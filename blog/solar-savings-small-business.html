Real Electricity Rates: Texas vs. Florida

Your potential savings depend heavily on where your business is located. Typical commercial rates (these vary by utility and rate plan):

  • Texas: Commercial rates average 8–12 cents per kWh, among the lowest in the country thanks to the deregulated ERCOT market. A small business using 2,500 kWh/month pays roughly $200–$300 before fees.
  • Florida: Commercial rates run somewhat higher, typically 10–14 cents per kWh. Combined with Florida's high air conditioning loads, many small businesses see larger absolute savings from solar despite the higher baseline.

Both states benefit from genuinely strong solar production — abundant sun hours mean a smaller system produces more usable electricity than the same system would in a cloudier state, improving the math on both sides.

What Determines Your Payback Period?

  • Your current monthly bill. Higher bills mean more absolute dollar savings, which speeds up payback in raw terms even though the percentage saved stays similar.
  • Roof orientation and shading. South-facing roofs with no shade produce the most energy per panel, directly improving your numbers.
  • Financing choice. A cash purchase has the fastest true payback. A $0-down solar loan spreads the cost out but often creates immediate monthly savings from day one since the loan payment is structured below your old bill.
  • The 30% federal tax credit. While it lasts, this single incentive cuts your net system cost by nearly a third, which shortens payback meaningfully.

The 30% Federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC)

This is the single biggest lever for small business solar economics. Your business receives a direct 30% tax credit — not a deduction — against the full installed cost of your system. A $100,000 system becomes a genuine $70,000 net cost after the credit is applied. Under current law the 30% rate applies to systems placed in service by December 31, 2027 (or to projects that began construction by July 4, 2026, which have longer to finish) — so timing is a real consideration. Talk to a tax professional before you decide.

A Real-World Example

Consider a Texas retail store paying $1,800/month in electricity — about 18,000 kWh at roughly 10¢/kWh. A system sized to offset 70% of that would be around 100 kW and, at $1.80–$2.55 per watt, cost roughly $180,000–$255,000 before incentives, or about $126,000–$179,000 after the 30% ITC. Savings of roughly $1,260/month (about $15,000/year) put simple payback at around 8–12 years before depreciation and financing effects. A smaller, roof-limited system costs less and offsets less. Your quotes will show real numbers.

⚡ Pro tip: Businesses with high daytime energy use — restaurants, retail stores, salons, offices with regular hours — tend to see stronger payback, since solar production peaks exactly when their consumption peaks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a small business pay for electricity per month?

In Texas, small businesses typically pay $200 to $800 per month for electricity at rates between 8 and 12 cents per kWh. In Florida, average commercial rates are slightly higher, often in the 10 to 14 cents per kWh range.

What is a typical solar payback period for a small business?

Payback varies widely with your electricity rate, system cost and tax situation. On bill savings plus the 30% federal tax credit alone, simple payback for many Texas and Florida small businesses works out to roughly 7–12 years; depreciation, financing and higher rates can shorten it. After payback, the system keeps producing electricity for the rest of its 25+ year life.

How much is the federal solar tax credit for businesses?

The federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) can provide a 30% tax credit on qualifying commercial solar systems. Under current law, systems that did not begin construction by July 4, 2026 must be placed in service by December 31, 2027 to qualify. Confirm eligibility with a tax professional.

Do I need to own my building to install solar?

No, though it's simpler if you do. Many tenants negotiate a Solar Rider Agreement with their landlord that allows them to install and claim incentives on a leased property. Get quotes first so you have real numbers to bring to that conversation.
