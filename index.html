Why Solar Makes Sense

Your Electricity Bill Is a
Profit Killer. Solar Fixes That.

Commercial electricity rates can climb over time. Solar can lock in the cost of a large share of your electricity for 25+ years.

💰

Immediate Monthly Savings

Savings start once your system is operating. As an illustration, a restaurant paying $3,000/month might offset $1,200–$2,100 of that bill, depending on how much solar its roof can host.

🏛️

30% Federal Tax Credit

The federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) can cover 30% of a qualifying system’s cost — $15,000 on a $50,000 system. Timing matters: systems that did not start construction by July 4, 2026 must be placed in service by December 31, 2027. Confirm eligibility with a tax professional.

📈

May Add Property Value

Owned solar can add to a building’s value, but results vary by market and system. Ask a local appraiser before counting on it.

Where We Operate

Texas & Florida —
Two of the Best Solar States in the US

High electricity rates, abundant sunshine, and strong incentive programs make both states exceptional for commercial solar ROI.

🤠

Texas

Texas led the US in new solar capacity added in 2025 (SEIA / Wood Mackenzie), and its large installer market means plenty of competition for your business. Payback depends heavily on your electricity rate and tax situation.

#1 state for new solar capacity added in 2025
Commercial electricity: roughly 8–12¢/kWh (varies by provider)
Large, competitive installer market
Cities: Houston, Dallas, Austin, San Antonio
🌴

Florida

The Sunshine State lives up to its name. Florida offers a sales-tax exemption on solar equipment, a property-tax exemption for commercial solar, and net metering through its utilities — all worth confirming for your situation.

Sales-tax exemption on solar equipment
80% property-tax exemption for commercial solar (through 2037)
Net metering available (rules vary by utility)
Cities: Miami, Tampa, Orlando, Jacksonville
